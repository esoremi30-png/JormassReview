What "international standard" actually means here

This isn't a subjective polish question — bodies like DOAJ (Directory of Open Access Journals) and COPE (Committee on Publication Ethics) publish concrete criteria that reputable journals are expected to meet on their websites. The redesign below is built around closing those specific gaps, not just making the page prettier:

Content architecture

The current site is essentially one long homepage with a thin "About" cluster behind it. An international-standard journal site separates discovery (find and cite an article), trust (verify the journal is legitimate), and participation (submit or review) into distinct, well-signposted areas.

Why split "About" into five pages instead of one

Each of those five topics (scope, board, review process, ethics, licensing) is something a specific visitor type is scanning for in isolation — a prospective author checking ethics policy has a different need than a librarian checking licensing. One long page forces everyone to scroll past what they don't need.

Why indexing gets its own page instead of homepage logos

Logos alone (as on the current site) are unverifiable — anyone can paste a Crossref logo. Linking each badge to the journal's live, external listing on that index turns a claim into something a skeptical reader can check in one click.

Why the editorial calendar replaces static announcements

The current "Call for Papers" is a single hand-edited paragraph that quietly went stale. A calendar of submission and publication dates, updated once per cycle rather than freehand, is far less likely to drift out of sync with reality.

Redesign preview: homepage

Design direction ties the visual identity to something true about the journal rather than a generic academic template: this is a management-and-social-science journal published by a college of an agricultural university. Signature element

The corner spine-tab reading the current volume and issue number is the one recurring motif carried across every page — a nod to the physical bound-journal spine you'd find on a library shelf, reinterpreted as a fixed wayfinding marker so a reader always knows exactly which issue they're in, without it turning into decoration for its own sake.

Updated verdict

B+

With this architecture and identity in place, JORMASS would present itself the way its actual scholarship deserves: as a legitimate, actively stewarded regional journal, verifiable in a few clicks rather than taken on faith. The underlying editorial and peer-review practices still need to genuinely match what the site now claims — a redesign can't manufacture rigor it can only stop hiding it.
